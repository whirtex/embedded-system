\section{Introduction}

Maintenance of air-conditioning units is still predominantly corrective or calendar-based, which yields inspections that reveal the condition of the equipment only at isolated moments and do not track degradation between visits. Embedded systems and Internet of Things (IoT) technologies can complement these inspections through continuous, non-invasive measurements of compressors and fan motors. Yousuf et al. demonstrate a low-cost architecture combining temperature, vibration, current, voltage, and speed sensors, an Arduino-based acquisition unit, GSM alerts, and remote storage on the Blynk platform. Mohammed et al. likewise developed a sensor network with a Raspberry Pi and MQTT communication for continuous acquisition of vibration, current, and temperature in operating electric motors \cite{yousuf2024iot,mohammed2023iot}.

A more specific research challenge is to infer compressor condition from measurements that can be collected without disassembling or interrupting the equipment. Environmental quantities provide important context, but they do not by themselves capture the vibrational and electrical signals associated with imbalance, bearing wear, and overcurrent. Kolok et al. investigated a low-cost ESP32 node with MEMS vibration and acoustic sensors, extracting RMS and FFT features and applying an Isolation Forest model to detect deviations from healthy operation. Their prototype achieved an overall detection accuracy above 72.9\%. Mohammed et al. compared five supervised algorithms on data from an AC motor under normal and fault conditions, with Random Forest achieving the highest reported accuracy. Together, these studies motivate an embedded and IoT solution that combines vibration, current, and temperature sensing with lightweight processing, while requiring validation under the on/off cycling and thermal-load variation typical of refrigeration systems \cite{kolok2025lowcost,mohammed2023iot}.

\textit{Related work.} Predictive maintenance aims to anticipate equipment failures from operational evidence so that maintenance actions can be planned before breakdowns occur. Effective systems therefore depend not only on the selected model, but also on the organization and quality of the complete data pipeline. Meitz et al. organized 249 publications into a framework of nine categories and 73 attributes, covering condition monitoring, fault detection, degradation modeling, scheduling, data handling, prognostic techniques, and evaluation. Gupta et al. complemented this broad view with a field study of airport baggage-handling conveyors, where vibration data were collected from a live system despite the absence of historical run-to-failure records. Their pipeline distinguished anomalies from noisy observations, processed heterogeneous maintenance records, and compared four classifiers. Random Forest achieved the best reported motor--gearbox classification performance, with precision, recall, and F1-score of 0.86. Together, these studies indicate that sensing, data cleaning, labeling, and model evaluation should be treated as connected parts of the proposed system \cite{meitz2025pdmframework,gupta2023baggage}.

Beyond predictive accuracy, maintenance systems must provide outputs that can be understood and acted upon by domain users. Burmeister et al. investigated this issue using 227,996 production and inspection observations from a large industrial-equipment manufacturer, with 29 variables describing the manufacturing process. The authors used Bayesian networks and classification trees to relate production errors to critical inspection errors. The resampling-based classification-tree configuration achieved the highest F1-score and accuracy among the evaluated models and translated the predictions into interpretable production rules. However, the case is tied to one manufacturing process and involves an imbalanced response, so external validation remains necessary. This study supports the use of interpretable alerts in the proposed project and shows that the system should explain which monitored conditions motivate a maintenance action instead of presenting only an isolated prediction \cite{burmeister2023production}.
